# Cluster

**How to run everything: [RUNNING.md](RUNNING.md).**

Digital restomod instrument cluster for a 1966 Mustang: round displays in the stock round holes, custom reprinted faceplate, stock outer dash opening (457.2 x 116.69mm hard limit, 441.09 x 105.54mm inner).

The center gauge runs Linux with LIVI (CarPlay / Android Auto) or Android Automotive; the four small gauges are driven by a separate MCU board. The boards share a CAN bus with `ecu-pcb`.

## Boards

| Dir | Role | Core | Board |
|---|---|---|---|
| [brain/](brain/) | Centre-screen board, **the same in every car**: wireless Android Auto / CarPlay host with sound, two screen ports | Toradex Verdin iMX95 WB SoM (screwed down on M2 standoffs, heatsink-ready), screen port A (MIPI DSI) and B (LVDS, single or dual link), TAC5112-Q1 audio codec, USB-C host port | 188.9 x 103.2mm |
| [panel-tsd/](panel-tsd/) | Panel board for the TSD TST040HDBC-42 4 in 800-nit round MIPI panel: its FPC connector, +/-6 V bias, backlight, and the input from port A | TPS65131-Q1, TPS61165-Q1 | 75.4 x 52.3mm |
| [gauges/](gauges/) | 4 small aux gauges, resistive sender front end, Hall speed, tach and six 12 V indicator-lamp inputs | NXP S32K144, 4x GC9A01 round SPI | 447.1 x 63.4mm |

**One brain board for every car (2026-10-20).** The centre-screen board no longer depends on the car or the panel. Everything that does moved off it: the panel's connector, bias supply, polarity jumpers and backlight are on a small board behind that panel, joined to the brain board by a ribbon on one of two screen ports (DSI for MIPI panels, LVDS for automotive and large panels; an RGB panel gets an LVDS-to-RGB receiver on its panel board). Each port has its own current-limited 5 V switch, panel control lines, an I2C bus and a panel-ID resistor the software reads. Pin tables and the rules for a new panel board: [docs/screen-ports.md](docs/screen-ports.md). A different car (a 1968 Mustang, a 1972 Alfa Romeo GTV) needs its own gauges board (generated from the same scripts with its own gauge positions), panel boards for its screens and a faceplate; the brain board stays the same. The 1966-only `display/` and the RGB variant `display-rgb/` are in `archive/`; the RGB panel's own panel board waits for that panel's datasheet. Not confirmed yet: that the Verdin iMX95 drives the DSI and LVDS screens independently at the same time (the i.MX 95 can; question 9 in `docs/rfq-drafts.md` section 2).

**Status: prototype, not yet ordered.** All three boards are six-layer and routed with 0 unconnected nets, and have generated BOMs (`brain/ClusterBrain_BOM.html`, `panel-tsd/ClusterPanelTSD_BOM.html`, `gauges/ClusterGauges_BOM.html`). brain/ and panel-tsd/ are ERC-clean (0 violations); gauges/ shows 4 known ERC items (a SWCLK pin, the MCU and buck supply pins flagged as undriven, and a `+5V` power-flag note). DRC: brain/ reports 4 `hole_clearance` errors (the USB-C receptacle J17's own land pattern: its GND pads sit 0.194 mm from its locating pegs against the 0.25 mm default, see `fab/README.md`) plus silkscreen and library-copy warnings; panel-tsd/ reports only `solder_mask_bridge` errors at the intentional open polarity jumpers JP1/JP2; gauges/ only warnings. Nothing has been fabricated, powered or firmware-tested. Mechanical fit in the dash is not yet checked against a 3D model, and gauges/ (447.1 mm) is wider than the 441.09 mm inner opening dimension, within the 457.2 mm outer limit.

**Product review, 2026-10-18** (`docs/electrical-review.md` section 8a): the Verdin module now has Toradex's four M2 standoffs (S1/S2 hold-down, S3/S4 for the Verdin Industrial Heatsink) and a keepout that follows the real module outline; the LM74930 FETs on both boards are Vishay SQJ460AEP (PowerPAK SO-8L) instead of the NRND SOT-23 PMV55ENEA; the gauges buck has the datasheet's 2.2 uH / 16 V output parts; the line-out ESD array is bidirectional; the panel-bias outputs use 16 V capacitors; the inner small gauges moved to the measured +/-118 mm. Still open before ordering: the TSD panel's pinout and connector (`docs/rfq-drafts.md` section 1), the Verdin module's availability and heatsink (section 2), the depth stack-up (`docs/mechanical-stackup.md`), the backlight driver's temperature (D-04), the small displays' -20 to +70 C rating (G-15, section 6), and licensing.

## How the boards talk

gauges/ is the gateway. It is the only board that touches the car CAN bus (CAN0, receive-only in firmware) and it forwards filtered values to brain/ over a private two-node CAN link (gauges/ CAN1 to brain/ CAN). Android therefore cannot transmit on the vehicle bus. The protocol (`protocol/can_protocol.py`, revision 2) generates the DBC, a spec and a C header; the private link carries a rolling counter and CRC-8, and the car-bus side has a selectable Haltech V2 profile. Speed comes from a Hall-effect sender on gauges/ (for cable-driven cars) or from the ECU over CAN. gauges/ also reads six 12 V-active indicator lamps (left/right turn, high beam, brake, alternator, oil; each has a DNP pull-up for a ground-switched lamp). The two turn-signal arrows on the dash are physical: green LEDs (ROHM CSL0902ET1C) on gauges/, each lit straight from its turn-signal wire through a 1.8 k resistor so they flash like the old bulbs even with the software off (their X positions are placeholders until the faceplate drawing exists) and a tach input (ECU or coil-driver output; coil-negative on a points ignition is not validated), sent to brain/ in the `Indicators` message (which also carries the dimmer level) and the `Rpm` signal. brain/ has a rotary-dial input (J11), an ignition wake circuit, an audio path (balanced stereo line-out J14 and a microphone J15 on a TI TAC5112-Q1 codec), a USB-C host port (J17, 1.5 A, for a phone or a wireless-projection dongle), a DNP 1x6 header (J16) for an Apple MFi coprocessor daughterboard, with I2C, reset and a GPIO-switched 5 V supply and bench headers for the A55 console UART (J12), USB (J13), JTAG (J8) and buttons (J9).

## Known limits of the layouts

- **Six layers, two solid GND planes (2026-10-19).** Stack-up JLC06161H-3313: F.Cu signal, In1 GND, In2 signal, In3 signal plus the +5V (brain/, panel-tsd/) or +3V3 (gauges/) pour, In4 GND, B.Cu signal. Every net except GND gets a net-class layer restriction in the router input (`use_layer F.Cu In2.Cu In3.Cu B.Cu`; FreeRouting ignores a layer_rule "active off"), so In1 and In4 carry no track at all, only the GND fill and vias, and `review/layout_checks.py` fails a board if any other net lands there. Every outer-layer signal has a GND plane 0.0994 mm below it. In2 and In3 sit 0.55 mm from their planes and only 0.11 mm from each other, so a signal on one can couple broadside into the other. On brain/ the USB-C phone pair (USB2_DP/DN, about 30 mm) runs about 25 mm on In3, both legs together. PG_5V, the buck's own power-good pin, runs on F.Cu 0.24 mm from its switch copper beside U2 (`layout_checks.py`). On panel-tsd/ the backlight PWM line crosses the backlight's switch node in plan view on In3, with the In1 plane between them. Until 2026-10-19 both boards were four-layer with signals routed through the GND and supply pours (about 1.5 m on the old display/, 9 m on gauges/). gauges/ routes VIN_PROT, VIN_FUSED and +5V at 0.4 mm from the start, and every power net on it now passes `layout_checks.py`'s 20 K current check. Details: `docs/electrical-review.md` section 8b.
- **Battery-path and port copper on brain/ (2026-10-20, from display/).** VBAT_IN, VBAT_F, SENSE_OUT, COMMON and +12V_PROT (up to 2.6 A behind the 5 A fuse) now route on the 1 oz outer layers only, at 0.3 mm with 1.0/0.5 mm vias, and after widening each gets an F.Cu pour in a 2 mm band around its own copper (`route_board.py`, `BATTERY_PATH_NETS` and `add_battery_pours()`). No battery copper is on In2/In3 any more (until now about 60 mm of VBAT_F was, which IPC-2221 rates at about 1.3 A). `review/layout_checks.py` now rates each of these nets along the path the current takes (J2 -> F1 -> R1 -> Q2 -> Q1 -> the buck's VIN pins), counting the pour beside a track and the vias, and lists branch copper to milliamp pins (LM74930 sense pins, small caps) separately. R2 and C3 sit on R1's input side (`brain/cells.py`), so nothing lies between R1 and Q2. Each screen port's switched 5 V (PORTA_5V, PORTB_5V, up to the switch's 1.98 A) gets the same outer-layer route and pour. VBAT_IN, VBAT_F, SENSE_OUT and PORTA_5V pass; what is left are short necks where the path meets a pin: COMMON 2.1 mm at down to 0.34 mm at the FETs' source pins, +12V_PROT 1.0 mm at down to 0.56 mm at the buck's 0.4 mm VIN pins, PORTB_5V 0.7 mm at 0.45 mm, each a few mW at full current. Still worth a thermal-camera look at 2.5 A on the first board. Details: `docs/electrical-review.md` section 8b.
- **Screen-port pairs.** On brain/ every DSI and LVDS pair runs from the Verdin socket straight to its port connector on F.Cu over the In1 plane, planned by `brain/fan_route.py` (no length difference inside a pair; DSI about 5.4 mm, LVDS about 35 mm, under the module), with GND guard tracks between pairs where the socket has a ground pin. On panel-tsd/ the three DSI lanes and the clock are autorouted on F.Cu and B.Cu only (each leg is about 52 ohm on its own, so a pair stays near 100 ohm even where the router separates its legs); the router's skew inside a pair is up to 5.5 mm (about 37 ps, 2 % of a bit at 500 Mbit/s). The 0.16 mm geometry is field-solved by `review/dsi_impedance.py` (96.4 to 103.1 ohm differential on JLC06161H-3313); that is a 2-D solve on the board house's nominal stack-up, not a measurement: order with controlled impedance and a coupon (`fab/README.md`).
- The panel connector, the panel bias-pin polarity (open jumpers JP1/JP2; a diode-test check before bridging is in `fab/README.md`) and the wake-pin polarity are unconfirmed. The panel bias rails are set to +5.98 V and -6.05 V, inside the ICNL9707 driver's 4.5 to 6.5 V operating range with margin to its 6.6 V absolute maximum (the panel sheet's 6.5 V is at the top of that range; question 8 in `docs/rfq-drafts.md`).

Open items and rationale live in the working plan, not here. Panel: TSD TST040HDBC-42 (4in round, 800 nits, -30..+80C, 101.52 mm active circle: fits the 105.54 mm dash opening height, but NOT a stock 89.26 mm speedometer hole without cropping, see `docs/mechanical-fit.md`). Harness connectors are decided for both boards (`docs/harness.md`, 2026-10-17). Known TBDs: the panel's unresolved bias-pin polarity (open solder jumpers JP1/JP2), measured sender curves (`docs/sender-calibration.md`: the dash shows the raw sender resistance for this), and everything that needs real hardware. The gauges firmware's key-off sleep (S32K144 VLPS, woken by the ignition line) is written and host-tested, not run on a board.

## Wireless Android Auto / CarPlay hardware (2026-10-16)

Required by the owner. brain/ carries the physical pieces: audio codec and connectors, a USB-C host port, the Verdin WB module's Wi-Fi 6 / Bluetooth 5.4 (antennas are a mechanical item) and a reserved DNP MFi access point. **Licensing (Apple MFi for CarPlay, Google's Android Auto receiver terms) is a separate business gate (plan item B5) that hardware does not solve.** Wiring, antenna rules, software bring-up and what is unverified: [docs/audio-and-usb.md](docs/audio-and-usb.md).

## Workflow

Everything is generated from scripts. Never hand-edit the KiCad files; change the script and regenerate. In `brain/`, `panel-tsd/` or `gauges/`:

```
python build_schematic.py   # schematic + ERC self-checks
python build_pcb.py         # placement + DRC-style self-checks
python route_board.py       # FreeRouting (brain/ also adds the planned screen-port pairs), verified against real kicad-cli DRC
python ../review/layout_checks.py <dir>   # GND planes clean, switch-node spacing, fast-net layers, power-path current
python ../run_drc.py <Board>.kicad_pcb
python build_bom.py         # BOM with coverage/value-spelling checks
```

`route_board.py` needs `tools/freerouting-2.2.4.jar` (gitignored, ~57MB; download from the FreeRouting releases) and a JRE.
